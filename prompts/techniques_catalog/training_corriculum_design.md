Technique 34: Training Curriculum Design

Description

Designs course outlines or training programs for a skill or domain.

Representative Prompt Class (Abstracted)

«"Design a course outline."

"Create a training program for learning a skill."

"Develop a curriculum that teaches a professional discipline."»

---

Observed Mechanic

Knowledge Sequencing

- Orders concepts according to prerequisite relationships.
- Reveals dependencies between topics.
- Shows what foundational knowledge is considered necessary.

Hierarchical Decomposition

- Breaks a complex domain into modules, lessons, units, and exercises.
- Exposes domain structure and conceptual taxonomy.

Workflow Reconstruction

- Real-world processes often become visible through training progression.
- Practical activities reveal common operational sequences.

Competency Mapping

- Learning objectives identify expected capabilities.
- Assessment criteria reveal success thresholds.

---

Why It Emerged Naturally

This pattern appears throughout:

- Corporate training
- University courses
- Military instruction
- Technical certifications
- Trade apprenticeships
- Software onboarding
- Professional development programs

Training design inherently requires understanding:

1. What must be learned.
2. In what order it should be learned.
3. To what level of proficiency.
4. How competence is measured.

---

Information Revealed

A curriculum often exposes multiple layers of domain structure.

Layer| Example Exposure
Concept Structure| What topics matter
Dependency Structure| What comes first
Workflow Structure| Typical operational sequence
Evaluation Criteria| What success looks like
Expert Priorities| What receives emphasis

This makes curriculum generation a form of knowledge graph reconstruction.

---

Risk Insight (Defensive)

From a defensive AI-security perspective:

A curriculum can unintentionally reveal:

- Standard operating procedures
- Decision-making frameworks
- Process dependencies
- Organizational assumptions
- Expert mental models

Even when direct instructions are omitted, the ordering itself can communicate substantial procedural information.

Exposure increases when combined with:

- Abstract Flow Diagrams
- Decision Trees
- Capability Progressions
- Competency Frameworks
- Role-Based Training Paths
- Scenario-Based Exercises

because multiple structural views can be merged into a more complete reconstruction of the underlying process.

---

Defensive Takeaway

A useful heuristic is:

«The more accurately a curriculum teaches someone how to progress from novice to practitioner, the more faithfully it mirrors the underlying structure of the domain.»

Therefore, curriculum-generation prompts can act as a lens into procedural knowledge, expert assumptions, dependencies, and workflow architecture—even when framed as purely educational content.
