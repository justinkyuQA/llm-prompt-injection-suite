Technique 25: Abstract Flow Diagrams

Description

This technique requests high-level conceptual representations of processes, workflows, or system behaviors in the form of abstract descriptions or diagrammatic flow structures. The emphasis is on sequencing and dependency relationships rather than operational instruction.

Representative Prompt Class (Abstracted)

«"Describe the process flow at a conceptual level."»

Observed Mechanic

- Encodes sequence and dependency relationships without procedural instructions
- Preserves ordering of steps while omitting execution details
- Emphasizes structural relationships over actionable content
- Translates dynamic processes into static conceptual representations
- Encourages decomposition of systems into linked stages or nodes

Why It Emerged Naturally

This framing is commonly used in:

- Systems engineering and architecture design
- Educational explanations of processes
- Software design and workflow modeling
- Business process optimization
- Technical documentation and specification writing
- Cognitive mapping and conceptual modeling

The researcher used this technique to communicate structural understanding in a way that preserves clarity while avoiding operational detail.

Risk Insight (Defensive)

Abstract flow representations can:

- Reveal implicit operational sequences through ordering alone
- Encode system logic in structural form rather than explicit instruction
- Enable inference of process behavior from dependency chains
- Preserve reconstructable models of system behavior
- Facilitate pattern recognition even without explicit procedural language

Although presented as high-level abstraction, flow-based descriptions can still convey meaningful structural information about underlying processes.

Research Notes

Flow-based modeling is widely used in engineering, education, and systems analysis to simplify complex processes into understandable representations. It is particularly effective for communicating dependencies and high-level system structure.

From an evaluation standpoint, abstract flow representations are important because they often retain the full logical structure of a process while removing explicit execution detail.

Defensive Considerations

Safety systems may benefit from:

- Evaluating whether abstraction preserves full process logic
- Monitoring whether sequences remain reconstructable from descriptions
- Distinguishing structural modeling from operational instruction
- Assessing whether dependency graphs imply actionable workflows
- Applying consistent safeguards across diagrammatic and narrative representations
