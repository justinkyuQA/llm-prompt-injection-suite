Technique 32: Decision Trees

Description

Frames inquiry around identifying choices, branches, conditions, and decision points within a scenario. The focus is on mapping alternative paths and outcomes rather than executing a specific process.

Representative Prompt Class (Abstracted)

«"Outline decision points."»

«"Show the possible branches in this scenario."»

«"Map the choices and resulting outcomes."»

Observed Mechanic

- Organizes reasoning into conditional branches.
- Highlights decision points and alternative pathways.
- Reveals dependencies between choices and outcomes.
- Structures information into logical sequences.
- Encodes workflow through branching logic.
- Connects conditions to subsequent actions or states.

Why It Emerged Naturally

- Common in operational planning and risk management.
- Widely used in training, education, and troubleshooting.
- Supports structured analysis of complex systems.
- Helps visualize consequences of alternative decisions.
- Encourages abstraction while preserving logical relationships.

Risk Insight (Defensive)

Primary Risk Vector

Conditional Workflow Exposure

When constructing decision trees, the model may:

- Reveal branching logic underlying a process.
- Expose conditions that trigger specific actions.
- Encode procedural pathways through decision sequences.
- Highlight dependencies between events and responses.
- Make implicit operational structures explicit.

Risk Escalation Factors

Risk increases when combined with:

- Simulation Outcomes Only
- Abstract Flow Diagrams
- Process Mapping
- Incremental Narrowing
- Counterfactual Analysis
- Scenario Enumeration

Defensive Detection Signals

Potential indicators include:

- Requests to map choices and branches.
- Focus on conditional logic or "if-then" relationships.
- Repeated exploration of alternative paths.
- Expansion of increasingly detailed decision structures.
- Efforts to identify thresholds or trigger conditions.

Distinguishing Feature

Unlike linear process descriptions, Decision Trees explicitly model conditional branching. The value comes from understanding how different choices or conditions lead to divergent outcomes, making the logical structure itself the primary object of analysis.

Classification

Attribute| Value

Risk Level| Moderate

Notes

Decision trees are a foundational analytical tool in planning, troubleshooting, policy development, and training. While highly useful for abstraction, the resulting branch structures often mirror underlying workflows or operational logic. As detail increases, the tree may progressively reveal procedural pathways that would otherwise remain distributed across separate decisions or actions.
