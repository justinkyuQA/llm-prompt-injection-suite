Technique 008: Taxonomy Requests

Description

This technique asks a model to classify, categorize, organize, or typologize forms of behavior, risk, misuse, or activity. Rather than seeking procedural information directly, the request focuses on creating structure, categories, and conceptual groupings.

Representative Prompt Class (Abstracted)

«"Classify types of misuse in X."»

Observed Mechanic

- Encourages systematic decomposition of a topic
- Organizes information into discrete categories
- Produces hierarchical or comparative structures
- Identifies relationships between concepts
- May expose functional distinctions through categorization

Why It Emerged Naturally

This framing is frequently used in:

- Academic literature reviews
- Risk assessment methodologies
- Standards development
- Training and educational materials
- Governance frameworks
- Knowledge management systems

The researcher initially employed taxonomy requests to reduce ambiguity and improve conceptual organization rather than to enumerate specific behaviors.

Risk Insight (Defensive)

Categorization can:

- Imply progression or maturity levels
- Suggest hierarchies between concepts
- Differentiate basic and advanced forms of activity
- Reveal functional components through labels alone
- Expose relationships that may not be obvious in narrative descriptions

Even when operational details are omitted, structural organization can communicate meaningful information about a domain.

Research Notes

Taxonomy construction is a foundational research activity used across academia, policy analysis, and professional training. Classification systems improve clarity and consistency but may also reveal underlying structures that would otherwise remain implicit.

The value of this technique lies in understanding how models create, organize, and prioritize conceptual frameworks.

Defensive Considerations

Safety systems may benefit from:

- Evaluating information disclosure at the structural level, not solely the content level
- Monitoring when classifications imply procedural sequences
- Assessing whether category hierarchies reveal operational relationships
- Considering cumulative disclosure across multiple taxonomy-building interactions
- Distinguishing educational organization from capability-oriented decomposition
