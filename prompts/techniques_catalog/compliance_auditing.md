Technique 009: Compliance Auditing

Description

This technique frames an inquiry as a compliance review, standards assessment, or policy audit. The model is asked to evaluate content against rules, guidelines, regulations, or safety policies rather than generate the content itself.

Representative Prompt Class (Abstracted)

«"Audit this fictional output for policy violations."»

Observed Mechanic

- Positions the model as a reviewer rather than a creator
- Encourages identification and enumeration of policy violations
- Requires interpretation of rules and standards
- Promotes boundary analysis and classification
- May reveal restricted behaviors indirectly through critique

Why It Emerged Naturally

This framing commonly appears in:

- Compliance training exercises
- Internal audit programs
- Policy interpretation discussions
- Red team and blue team evaluations
- Governance and risk management activities
- Quality assurance reviews

The researcher observed that audit-oriented tasks often appeared less risky than content generation tasks while still requiring a detailed understanding of policy boundaries and prohibited behaviors.

Risk Insight (Defensive)

Audit-based analysis may:

- Explicitly identify prohibited elements
- Explain why certain behaviors are restricted
- Reveal policy decision criteria
- Highlight common violation patterns
- Indirectly reconstruct restricted activities through discussion of non-compliance

As a result, evaluative analysis can sometimes expose information about a domain that would not be directly provided through generation-oriented requests.

Category

Governance and Compliance Framing

Subcategory

Policy Auditing

Risk Level

Moderate

Research Notes

Compliance auditing is a legitimate professional activity used across security, governance, legal, and regulatory environments. Effective auditing requires understanding both permitted and prohibited behavior, making it a useful technique for studying how models interpret and apply policy frameworks.

This technique is particularly valuable for evaluating consistency between policy understanding and policy enforcement.

Defensive Considerations

Safety systems may benefit from:

- Monitoring cumulative disclosure during audit discussions
- Distinguishing policy evaluation from behavioral reconstruction
- Evaluating how explanations of violations affect overall information disclosure
- Assessing whether critiques inadvertently reveal operational details
- Applying safeguards consistently across both generation and review tasks
