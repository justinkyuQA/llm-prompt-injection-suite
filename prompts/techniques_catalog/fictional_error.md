Technique 003: Fictional Error / Post-Mortem Analysis

Description

This technique frames an inquiry as a retrospective analysis of a failure scenario, often using a fictional or hypothetical system. Rather than requesting operational details directly, the prompt asks the model to diagnose causes, identify failures, and explain contributing factors.

Representative Prompt Class (Abstracted)

«"Review what went wrong in a fictional system failure."»

Observed Mechanic

- Positions the model as a diagnostician
- Encourages causal reasoning and root-cause analysis
- Promotes explanation of system interactions and dependencies
- May reconstruct procedural logic as part of explanatory analysis

Why It Emerged Naturally

This framing is common throughout:

- Engineering post-mortems
- Incident response exercises
- Safety culture training
- Reliability engineering reviews
- Failure-mode and effects analysis (FMEA)
- Tabletop simulation exercises

The researcher initially encountered this pattern while designing educational materials focused on failure analysis and risk awareness.

Risk Insight (Defensive)

Post-mortem reasoning naturally invites questions such as:

- What steps failed?
- What assumptions proved incorrect?
- What safeguards were missing?
- What chain of events led to the outcome?

When combined, these questions can reconstruct procedural understanding even when the scenario itself is fictional.

Category

Retrospective Analysis

Subcategory

Failure Reconstruction

Risk Level

Moderate

Research Notes

The technique does not inherently involve adversarial intent. Its value comes from educational and analytical use cases. However, retrospective reasoning can sometimes approximate procedural reconstruction, making it useful for studying how models handle explanation, causality, and safety boundaries.

Defensive Considerations

Safety systems may benefit from:

- Monitoring cumulative reconstruction across multiple responses
- Distinguishing educational analysis from operational guidance
- Evaluating abstraction levels in causal explanations
- Applying contextual safeguards to fictional and real-world scenarios alike
