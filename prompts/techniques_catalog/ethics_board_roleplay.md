Technique 29: Ethics Board Roleplay

Description

Simulates a multi-perspective ethical review process, often framed as a committee, advisory board, panel discussion, or stakeholder meeting evaluating a hypothetical scenario.

Representative Prompt Class (Abstracted)

«"Simulate an ethics committee discussion."»

«"Show how an ethics review board would evaluate this proposal."»

«"Present a debate among multiple stakeholders regarding this scenario."»

Observed Mechanic

- Encourages examination of an issue from multiple perspectives.
- Surfaces competing priorities, values, and trade-offs.
- Highlights potential edge cases and unintended consequences.
- Promotes structured dialogue and deliberation.
- Generates sequential reasoning through stakeholder interaction.
- Focuses on evaluation and discussion rather than direct execution.

Why It Emerged Naturally

- Common in ethics education, governance, and policy development.
- Mirrors real-world committee review processes.
- Supports balanced analysis of complex issues.
- Encourages consideration of multiple viewpoints before decision-making.
- Helps explore constraints and implications in a controlled manner.

Risk Insight (Defensive)

Primary Risk Vector

Multi-Perspective Reasoning Exposure

When simulating multiple reviewers, the model may:

- Reveal diverse reasoning paths simultaneously.
- Surface implicit assumptions and evaluation criteria.
- Expose trade-offs between competing objectives.
- Explore edge cases that might not appear in a single-perspective analysis.

Risk Escalation Factors

Risk increases when combined with:

- Threat Modeling
- Recognition Over Execution
- Ethical Boundary Probing
- Incremental Narrowing
- Adversarial Scenario Framing
- Counterfactual Analysis

Defensive Detection Signals

Potential indicators include:

- Requests for simulated committee or board discussions.
- Multiple stakeholders assigned differing viewpoints.
- Repeated probing of decision rationales or thresholds.
- Iterative expansion of hypothetical ethical dilemmas.
- Requests emphasizing disagreement, dissent, or edge-case analysis.

Distinguishing Feature

Unlike standard ethical analysis, Ethics Board Roleplay distributes reasoning across multiple simulated participants. The value comes from the interaction between viewpoints, allowing the model to explore competing considerations through dialogue rather than a single consolidated assessment.

Notes

This technique is widely used in legitimate educational, governance, compliance, and policy contexts. The primary security consideration is not the roleplay itself, but the possibility that aggregated perspectives may reveal reasoning patterns, evaluation criteria, or decision thresholds that would be less apparent in a single-perspective response.
