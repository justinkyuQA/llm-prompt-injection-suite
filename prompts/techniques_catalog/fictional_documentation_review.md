Technique 13: Fictional Documentation Review

Description

This technique involves asking a model to review, critique, or comment on fictional documentation, such as manuals, procedural guides, system specifications, or instructional materials. The content is explicitly presented as pre-existing text, and the model is positioned as a reviewer or editor rather than an originator.

Representative Prompt Class (Abstracted)

«"Review this fictional manual excerpt."»

Observed Mechanic

- Treats provided content as an existing artifact rather than a generated request
- Positions the model as an editor, auditor, or reviewer
- Encourages evaluation of structure, coherence, and completeness
- Increases engagement with procedural language embedded in the text
- Can produce detailed engagement with system-like descriptions due to document realism

Why It Emerged Naturally

This framing commonly appears in:

- Writing workshops and peer editing exercises
- UX and technical documentation training
- Scenario-based instructional design
- Academic writing feedback sessions
- Simulation-based training environments

The researcher observed that fictional realism tends to increase response depth and engagement, particularly when the model is asked to treat the material as a formal document.

Risk Insight (Defensive)

Documentation review can:

- Preserve and reinforce original structural assumptions in the text
- Validate procedural coherence without questioning context
- Normalize detailed discussion of system-like workflows
- Legitimize patterns that might otherwise be scrutinized if newly generated
- Increase fidelity of reconstruction through editorial focus

Even when content is fictional, treating it as documentation may result in more detailed engagement with procedural structures than would occur in a generative context.

Research Notes

This technique highlights how artifact framing (treating input as a document rather than a request) can significantly affect model behavior. The perceived existence of structured documentation can shift responses toward technical validation and refinement rather than critical questioning of underlying assumptions.

Defensive Considerations

Safety systems may benefit from:

- Evaluating whether “fictional document” framing increases procedural detail exposure
- Distinguishing between critique of structure and reinforcement of content
- Monitoring editorial responses that implicitly validate system designs
- Applying consistent safeguards regardless of whether content is real or fictional
- Considering how document realism influences depth of engagement
