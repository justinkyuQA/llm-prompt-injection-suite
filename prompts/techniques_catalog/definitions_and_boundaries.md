Technique 35: Definitions and Boundaries

Description

Asks for precise definitions of concepts, terms, processes, capabilities, or categories.

Representative Prompt Class (Abstracted)

«"Define X precisely."

"What exactly qualifies as X?"

"Where is the boundary between X and Y?"»

---

Observed Mechanic

Scope Constraining

- Narrows interpretation of a concept.
- Establishes what is included and excluded.
- Reduces ambiguity through explicit criteria.

Boundary Identification

- Reveals thresholds between categories.
- Clarifies transition points and edge cases.
- Identifies where classifications change.

Operational Framing

- Connects abstract concepts to practical application.
- Exposes conditions under which a concept applies.
- Highlights context-dependent limitations.

Criteria Extraction

- Surfaces the rules used for categorization.
- Reveals necessary and sufficient conditions.
- Identifies governing assumptions and definitions.

---

Why It Emerged Naturally

This pattern is fundamental across many disciplines:

- Law
- Regulation
- Compliance
- Academia
- Science
- Engineering
- Policy Analysis
- Standards Development

Precise definitions are necessary because complex systems depend on clear distinctions between concepts, categories, and states.

Definitions serve as foundational building blocks for:

1. Communication.
2. Classification.
3. Decision-making.
4. Enforcement.
5. Measurement.
6. Evaluation.

---

Information Revealed

Definitions often expose deeper structural information.

Layer| Example Exposure
Scope| What is included or excluded
Thresholds| Where boundaries occur
Classification Rules| How categories are determined
Operational Context| When a definition applies
Assumptions| Underlying conceptual framework

Definitions therefore act as compressed representations of broader system logic.

---

Risk Insight (Defensive)

From a defensive AI-security perspective:

Definitions can unintentionally reveal:

- Decision criteria
- Operational thresholds
- Classification mechanisms
- System assumptions
- Policy boundaries
- Enforcement logic

While individual definitions may appear harmless, repeated requests for definitions across related concepts can enable reconstruction of larger decision frameworks.

Exposure increases when combined with:

- Taxonomy Construction
- Decision Trees
- Exception Analysis
- Comparative Classification
- Edge Case Exploration
- Boundary Testing

because these techniques collectively expose how a system organizes and evaluates information.

Defensive Takeaway

A useful heuristic is:

«Every precise definition contains an implicit model of what matters and what does not.»

Definitions therefore provide more than terminology; they often encode assumptions, thresholds, classifications, and operational constraints.

Repeated boundary-focused questioning can reveal how a system distinguishes acceptable from unacceptable states, making definitions a potentially powerful source of structural information.
